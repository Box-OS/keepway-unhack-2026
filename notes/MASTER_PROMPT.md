## PART 2 — ENGINEERING SOLUTION A: ELEVATED ALTERNATIVE ACCESS ROAD

### Concept

Construct a new alternative transportation route, preferably on higher and less flood-prone ground, to remain usable when existing roads become inundated or structurally damaged.

The alternative might be a newly aligned road, an elevated roadway, an embankment, an upgraded existing bypass or an elevated causeway supported by engineered drainage structures.

### Required analysis

For each community, investigate:

1. Existing road alignment and damaged sections.
2. Surrounding topography and elevation profiles.
3. Historical maximum floodwater elevations.
4. Flood return periods and projected climate-adjusted conditions.
5. Suitable higher-ground route options.
6. Distance and alignment of potential new routes.
7. Expected additional travel time.
8. Required elevation of the new roadway.
9. Embankment height and earthwork volumes.
10. Required culverts, bridges and drainage crossings.
11. Ground stability, erosion and scour hazards.
12. Construction accessibility and land requirements.
13. Road design standards, emergency-vehicle access and load capacity.
14. Environmental and cultural constraints.
15. Long-term maintenance costs and reliability.

### Engineering calculations

Where sufficient information exists, estimate:

- Proposed road length (km)
- Roadway width (m)
- Design road elevation (m above an identified vertical datum)
- Required road raising (m)
- Freeboard above design floodwater (m)
- Excavation and fill volumes (m³)
- Embankment footprint (m²)
- Culvert/bridge quantities and preliminary dimensions
- Expected access availability during design floods
- Construction material quantities

Do not confuse elevation above sea level with elevation above the floodwater surface.

Do not assume the highest available ground is automatically the optimal alignment. Evaluate road length, cost, drainage continuity, environmental effects and constructability.

If the community is surrounded by low-lying flooded terrain, determine whether building a separate road is realistic.

For Kashechewan, distinguish maintaining access **within the community and to the airport** from constructing a completely new year-round regional road.

## PART 3 — ENGINEERING SOLUTION B: FLOOD DIVERSION CHANNEL AND WATER STORAGE

### Concept

Construct an engineered flood-diversion channel that intercepts some of the floodwater threatening critical roads and redirects it to a designated detention basin, retention pond, reservoir, natural storage area, or other appropriately engineered storage facility.

The objective is to reduce flood depths, flow velocities, erosion or inundation duration at vulnerable transportation corridors.

### Required analysis

For each community, investigate:

1. Source and mechanism of flooding.
2. Watershed and upstream drainage characteristics.
3. Floodwater volume and peak discharge.
4. Water levels and available hydraulic head.
5. Whether gravity-driven diversion is possible.
6. Potential diversion channel alignment.
7. Channel gradient, length and cross-sectional geometry.
8. Required channel capacity.
9. Available storage locations and their elevations.
10. Required storage volume for the selected design event.
11. Land area required for storage.
12. Existing rivers, wetlands and drainage networks.
13. Potential return flow, overtopping and downstream impacts.
14. Sediment, debris, ice and blockage risks.
15. Pumping requirements if gravity drainage is inadequate.
16. Environmental permits, fisheries impacts and land rights.
17. Construction and maintenance costs.
18. Long-term performance during repeated or prolonged flooding.

### Engineering calculations

Estimate, where technically justified:

- Design inflow and peak discharge (m³/s)
- Diversion flow (m³/s)
- Channel length (m)
- Channel width and depth (m)
- Channel slope
- Expected water velocity (m/s)
- Required storage volume (m³)
- Storage area (hectares)
- Excavation volume (m³)
- Embankment and lining quantities
- Hydraulic control structures
- Pumping power and operating cost, if applicable
- Estimated reduction in roadway flood level (m)

Use accepted hydrologic and hydraulic methods, such as continuity, Manning's equation and flood hydrograph routing, where justified.

**Critical feasibility check:** Do not assume there is an available storage basin capable of holding a major flood.

Verify that the receiving area has sufficient volume, is hydraulically accessible and would not simply release water back toward the affected community.

For ice-jam flooding, test whether a diversion would actually reduce water levels or whether ice and backwater effects would make the system ineffective.

Reject an option if it would transfer unacceptable flood risk to downstream communities, critical infrastructure, wetlands or culturally significant land.

Do not propose channelizing a major river without evaluating the full river system.
