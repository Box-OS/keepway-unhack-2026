Project scope
This updated database consolidates research about four Canadian First Nations communities with reported
flood-related transportation disruptions. It excludes the previously removed U.S. community and the B.C. Highway
8 reconstruction case.
Kashechewan First Nation - Ontario
English River First Nation / Patuanak - Saskatchewan
Minegoziibe Anishinabe - Manitoba
Potlotek First Nation - Nova Scotia
Evidence note. Reported observations, preliminary hypotheses and proposed design concepts are distinguished throughout.
“Not verified” does not establish that no permanent repair exists. All location-specific engineering dimensions and performance
claims require confirmation before design.
Evidence verification and supporting reporting
Kashechewan: the May 1, 2025 road-flooding timestamp is supported by contemporaneous community reporting. The
approximately 2,000 figure refers to the community population in 2025, not people confirmed stranded on the road. This
database does not chart the conflicting evacuation-count estimates in the submitted notes.
Minegoziibe: do not use the Manitoba $15.9 million figure as a Minegoziibe allocation. The October 21, 2026 Manitoba release
explicitly identifies Peguis First Nation and Fisher River Cree Nation.
Potlotek: the September 14, 2026 local report confirms the affected road section remained closed, that water/wastewater utilities
were under monitoring, and that an alternate route was advised. No opening timestamp is documented.
Supporting reporting: Kashechewan (May 1, 2025) | English River (May 5, 2026) | Minegoziibe (July 2, 2026) | Potlotek (Sep. 14,
2026) | Manitoba funding clarification.

Section 1 | Kashechewan First Nation, Ontario
Main engineering concern: Repeated spring breakup and ice-jam flooding threaten critical transportation access, including the
only road to the airport.
#
Research field
Consolidated information
1
Exact damaged road location
Kashechewan airport access road between the settlement and Kashechewan Airport. The
route flooded on May 1, 2025; the exact chainage, intersection and GPS coordinates are
unverified. Obtain road inventory, incident maps and satellite imagery.
2
Flood date and duration
The airport road was reported compromised and beginning to flood at about 7:13 a.m. on
May 1, 2025. Its reopening date/time and total impassability duration have not been
verified; request incident/airport records.
3
Rainfall and peak flow
This was a spring river-breakup/ice-jam flood, not an established rainfall-runoff event.
Obtain Albany River stage, discharge, ice-jam chronology and local high-water marks; no
verified road-specific peak stage/flow is available.
4
Road elevation and dimensions
Unknown. Obtain DEM/LiDAR and field/as-built measurements of road crown elevation,
width, embankment height, distance to river and 2025 high-water level.
5
Culvert or bridge characteristics
Unknown whether the affected reach uses culverts, a causeway or a bridge. Identify all
drainage crossings and obtain dimensions, invert levels and hydraulic capacities before
proposing replacements.
6
Damage type and probable cause
Inundation during elevated Albany River water levels associated with spring breakup and
reported ice jamming. Whether embankment erosion or local drainage capacity
contributed to failure is unverified; examine high-water marks and road condition.
7
People and essential services
affected
Approximately 2,000 community residents were reported in May 2025 (population
estimate, not road-specific exposed count). Airport access failed and helicopter transfers
via Fort Albany were required. Conflicting evacuation counts in supplied notes are not
chart-ready.
8
Alternative access and detour
time
No year-round outside road: the community ordinarily relies on air access and seasonal
winter-road connectivity. Helicopters ferried evacuees to Fort Albany after road
inundation. Obtain flight capacities, weather limits and transfer times.
9
Existing repairs and effectiveness
No confirmed completed permanent protection of the airport-access road. Request status
of dike/road/drainage and longer-term community relocation work from the Nation and
appropriate governments.
10
Proposed design target and
constraints
Assess raising/protecting airport access and preserving emergency aviation during
ice-jam high-water conditions. Determine design stage and freeboard; evaluate alongside
the Nation’s relocation priorities and environmental/remote-logistics constraints.
11
Historical flood frequency
Annual spring flood-related evacuations have been reported for more than a decade; this
is a reported pattern, not proof the airport road itself closes annually. Compile event
dates and closure/evacuation histories by cause.
12
Floodwater depth and velocity
No verified values for the airport access road.

Section 2 | English River First Nation / Patuanak, Saskatchewan
Main engineering concern: Highway 918 is a critical road connection with multiple flood-related washouts.
#
Research field
Consolidated information
1
Exact damaged road location
Highway 918 north of Highway 165 toward Patuanak. Reported damaged/closed points at
approximately 17, 29 and 40 km north of Highway 165; Saskatchewan legislative debate
cited five washout locations. Survey and confirm each chainage.
2
Flood date and duration
Highway 918 closed from May 1, 2026; cautious access reportedly resumed around May
7 (about six days). Exact opening timestamps and whether full service returned then are
unknown.
3
Rainfall and peak flow
Rapid spring snowmelt and high water were reported. No verified Highway 918
catchment peak discharge or site-specific rainfall value.
4
Road elevation and dimensions
No surveyed geometry available. The road base was described as approximately 50
years old. Obtain as-builts, LiDAR/DEM, pavement and embankment cross-sections at
each washout.
5
Culvert or bridge characteristics
Some culverts were described as compressed/poor, and emergency culverts were
installed. Obtain diameters, lengths, inverts, grades, inspection ratings and hydraulic
capacity for each failed crossing.
6
Damage type and probable cause
Multiple washouts and overtopping during rapid snowmelt and high water. Distinguish
culvert insufficiency, blockage, scour, saturated embankment failure and potential
channel changes at individual sites.
7
People and essential services
affected
About 500 residents in Patuanak were reported isolated because Highway 918 was the
only road link. Confirm actual households, school enrolment and daily traffic before
charting exposed road users.
8
Alternative access and detour
time
Highway 918 was described as Patuanak’s only road connection, with no practical road
detour during the event. Examine seasonal boat/ice-road/air alternatives without
assuming availability.
9
Existing repairs and effectiveness
Ministry crews installed emergency culverts and stockpiled gravel, restoring cautious
drivability. The permanent scope, repair costs, completion and post-repair condition are
not verified.
10
Proposed design target and
constraints
Assess flood-resilient culvert crossings, reconstructed embankments, scour protection
and selective roadway raising. Need design return period/discharge, freeboard, local
maintenance plan and a construction traffic strategy.
11
Historical flood frequency
A complete historical count of road-damaging floods is unavailable. The May 2026 flood
is documented.
12
Floodwater depth and velocity
No numerical values reported at individual washout sites.
13
Exposed population
Approximately 500 residents reportedly relied on the affected highway. Exact households
and school-aged population require verification.
14
Educational disruption
No reliable number of missed school days, affected students or cancelled transportation
services.

Section 3 | Minegoziibe Anishinabe, Manitoba
Main engineering concern: Regional flooding damaged road infrastructure and compromised access in multiple directions.
#
Research field
Consolidated information
1
Exact damaged road location
Highway 20 toward Camperville and regional routes toward Swan River, with regional
washouts. Exact road damage coordinates require Manitoba 511/closure bulletins and
maintenance inspection reports.
2
Flood date and duration
State of emergency declared in early July 2026. Expected restoration of access to homes
was 3–4 days, NOT an established start-to-end road closure duration; obtain Manitoba
511 and community update records.
3
Rainfall and peak flow
Flooding occurred during the 2026 Manitoba Parkland flood event. No site-specific rainfall
amount or peak discharge identified.
4
Road elevation and dimensions
Not available. Obtain road crown/embankment profile, carriageway width, shoulders and
adjacent floodplain elevations.
5
Culvert or bridge characteristics
Washed-out infrastructure documented, but individual culverts/bridges and dimensions
unknown. Obtain provincial bridge/culvert inventory and inspection reports.
6
Damage type and probable cause
Road washouts and damaged infrastructure during regional flooding. Forensic site review
must distinguish runoff/culvert overload, overtopping, embankment scour and slope
instability.
7
People and essential services
affected
Community access and connection to regional emergency services were cut off. The
affected resident and household counts are not established; collect First Nation and
provincial mobility data.
8
Alternative access and detour
time
Both main travel directions were reported disrupted. Map any viable detour and measure
distance, travel time, load restrictions and ambulance suitability.
9
Existing repairs and effectiveness
Emergency flood response and recovery were reported, but site-specific completed
repairs, costs and inspection outcomes are not verified. Do not attribute the province’s
$15.9m Peguis/Fisher River funding to Minegoziibe.
10
Proposed design target and
constraints
Investigate raising critical road sections, improving drainage and culverts, protecting
embankments and improving emergency access redundancy. Constraints include cost,
terrain and construction logistics.
11
Historical flood frequency
Complete community-specific flood-frequency record unavailable. Additional historical
flood events need to be compiled.
12
Floodwater depth and velocity
No verified numerical values for affected roads.
13
Exposed population
Exact number of households and residents dependent on the affected routes not
established in the research.
14
Educational disruption
No published figure for students affected, school days missed or school transportation
disruption.
15
Emergency response impact
Emergency-service access was reportedly interrupted. Ambulance response time and
evacuation delays not quantified.

Section 4 | Potlotek First Nation, Nova Scotia
Main engineering concern: Embankment damage, erosion and potentially inadequate drainage capacity along Chapel Island
Road.
#
Research field
Consolidated information
1
Exact damaged road location
Chapel Island Road / Sitmuk Awti near Matle’n’s Brook in Potlotek, Cape Breton. Exact
damaged chainage and embankment extents require the local engineering assessment;
route leads toward Mniku/Chapel Island.
2
Flood date and duration
September 14, 2026 report confirms the affected Chapel Island Road section was
CLOSED due to roadway and embankment damage. Start/end timestamps and total
closure duration remain unknown.
3
Rainfall and peak flow
Continued rainfall was reported, but no verified Matle’n’s Brook rainfall intensity/total,
recurrence interval or peak discharge. Obtain local ECCC observations and catchment
modelling.
4
Road elevation and dimensions
Not reported. Survey the road crown, embankment height and width, slope, local ground
and water levels near the affected culverts.
5
Culvert or bridge characteristics
Engineering assessment found existing culverts MAY be unable to convey the observed
flow. Obtain dimensions, inverts, slopes, blockage condition and capacity; insufficiency is
not yet proven.
6
Damage type and probable cause
Documented roadway/embankment damage and erosion from flooding. Test whether
culvert backup, overland runoff or tidal/coastal interaction drove the failure; inadequate
drainage is a hypothesis.
7
People and essential services
affected
Closure affected travel toward culturally important Mniku/Chapel Island; the adjacent
area also contains critical underground community water/wastewater lines under
monitoring. People and service interruption counts unknown.
8
Alternative access and detour
time
Officials instructed road users to take an alternate route; the detour distance, weather
reliability, road capacity and ambulance suitability are unverified. The community must
not be described as fully isolated.
9
Existing repairs and effectiveness
As of Sept. 14, 2026 the Nation sought emergency support and funding for permanent
engineered roadway/embankment stabilization, culvert improvements and drainage; no
verified completed permanent repair.
10
Proposed design target and
constraints
Assess appropriately sized culverts, embankment reconstruction, erosion protection, and
water/wastewater utility safeguarding; apply future-rainfall design and culturally
sensitive construction review.
11
Historical flood frequency
No complete historical flood record for Chapel Island Road or Matle’n’s Brook.
12
Floodwater depth and velocity
No verified measurements.
13
Exposed population
Number of residents, households, students and visitors dependent on the damaged road
not quantified.
14
Educational disruption
No reliable number of students or school days affected.
